Juee Jahagirdar

Final-year undergraduate, IIT Bombay  /  independent AI safety research

Most of what I do is empirical: train or probe a model, find where its behaviour comes apart, and work out whether that failure is visible from the inside. That has taken me through belief-state geometry in transformers, spectral structure in LLM activations, and backdoor propagation between agents.

My background is in mechanical engineering, and my first research was in dynamical systems. A lot of how I think about models still comes from there.

What I work on

Three threads that keep recurring across the work.

Interpretability and world models

Belief-state geometry in transformers, spectral signatures in LLM activations, and failure modes that turn out to depend on architecture rather than task.

Safety and threat models

Goal stability and distributional robustness as alignment-relevant properties; inner alignment failures in sequence predictors; the limits of monitoring across architectures.

Dynamical systems

Invariant set computation through RKHS variational optimisation, and attractor geometry learned directly from time-series data.
